Four things this plan is not

Not the provider's dates

Centenary sets the official programme calendar — the registered documents leave duration blank on purpose. Everything here is arithmetic on the registered credits, for planning. Confirm real dates with the provider before anything goes in a contract or a WSP.

Not a deadline

The programme is self-paced. Nothing here is enforced and nobody fails for drifting off it. Its whole purpose is to make drift visible early, while there is still time to do something about it.

Not the whole qualification

These eleven modules are the 80-credit knowledge component. The 13 practical modules and 4 workplace modules — the other 160 credits — run alongside as logbooks signed off by a mentor, on their own timeline.

Not a completion record

Finishing this plan is not the same as being found competent. Module assessments are marked by the provider, and the qualification is awarded only after the external assessment (EISA) set by the QCTO.

About notional hours. Ten hours per credit is how the NQF sizes a qualification — it covers everything a typical learner needs, including reading, activities, revision and assessment. It is a planning figure rather than a stopwatch: an experienced project administrator will move faster than someone new to the field, and Recognition of Prior Learning can remove whole modules from this plan. If the finish date above looks long, RPL and the Google pathway are the two levers that genuinely shorten it.
