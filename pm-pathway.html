Project Management pathway

Do the Google certificate first, and you may not have to study that theory again

The Google Project Management Certificate and our accredited NQF 5 qualification cover a lot of the same ground. This page sets out exactly how much — and, just as importantly, how much it doesn't — so you can plan around the real numbers instead of the hopeful ones.

Who this is for Anyone at M&T heading towards the Occupational Certificate: Project Manager, and any manager deciding how to get their team there. It is also the short version to give HR when you are asked why we would put someone through a free international course before an accredited one.
The qualification

240 credits, and only a third of them are theory

The Occupational Certificate: Project Manager is SAQA ID 101869, curriculum code 121905000, registered on the National Qualifications Framework at Level 5. It is built in three parts, and the proportions between them decide everything else on this page.

Knowledge — 80 credits

Eleven modules of theory, each assessed in its own right. This is the part an international course can reach, because it follows the international project management body of knowledge almost process for process.

Practical — 100 credits

Thirteen modules of applied project work, done under supervision. Initiating, planning, scheduling, costing, then monitoring and controlling a real project through to close-out.

Workplace — 60 credits

Four modules logged on real projects and evidenced in a portfolio. Not simulated, not a case study — the work you actually did, signed off by someone who watched you do it.

One credit is ten notional learning hours, so 240 credits is 2 400 hours. The Google certificate runs to roughly 142 hours. That comparison alone tells you the international course cannot be a substitute for the qualification — but it can still save a great deal of repeated work, which is the point of everything below.

The honest number

Centenary puts the overlap at about half the course content

Centenary Networks, the accredited provider, has confirmed that 50% of the course content matches the Google Career Certificates in Project Management. That figure is theirs, and theirs is the one that counts — they are the provider who decides what gets exempted. What follows is the module-by-module detail behind it: where the Google programme lands squarely on this curriculum, and where it does not reach at all.

Why you will not see a percentage next to each module below. Exemption is granted for a whole module or not at all — nobody is ever exempted 85% of a module. So the useful question is not how much of each module is covered, but which modules the Google programme genuinely reaches. That is what the two lists are, and they are where a conversation with the provider should start.

The bigger half

The 160 credits no course of any kind will give you

The practical and workplace modules are two-thirds of the qualification. No international certificate covers them, and neither does a classroom. They need supervised project work and logged workplace evidence — which, if you run projects at M&T, you are producing already.

You are generating the evidence now

Project charters, schedules, budgets, change records, risk registers, close-out reports. Every one of those is evidence against a named module, and most of it is already sitting in your email and on the server.

1 600 notional hours per person

That is what 160 credits works out to. Claimed against work you have genuinely done, rather than repeated as coursework — this is the largest saving available on this qualification, and it has nothing to do with Google.

The Google capstone helps, but it is not the portfolio

It produces a real charter, a quality evaluation and stakeholder reporting, and those are genuine artefacts worth keeping. The workplace modules still need evidence from real M&T projects, signed off here.

Someone has to sign it

Evidence has to be attributable to you and confirmed by a supervisor. Start keeping your own copies now, with dates and your name on them — it is far easier than reconstructing two years of projects later.

The route

How the 240 credits are actually earned

Three parts, in this order. The first one costs nothing, which is why it is worth starting before anything else is decided.

  1. Do the Google certificateSeven courses and a capstone, free to audit on Coursera. The certificate itself is paid, and Coursera offers financial aid. Completing it is what puts the overlap on the table — Centenary puts that at about half the course content.
  2. Put it forward for Recognition of Prior LearningThe certificate carries no NQF credits by itself. Credits come from being assessed against the module standards, with the Google certificate and your capstone work as part of the evidence. How RPL works
  3. Study the four local modules — 28 creditsEthics and the South African landscape, scheduling depth, quantitative risk, and procurement. Small, known, and taught here because nobody overseas teaches them.
  4. Claim the practical and workplace credits — 160 creditsRecognition of Prior Learning against real M&T project evidence, with support in building the portfolio. This is the long part, and the part worth the most.
  5. Sit the EISAThe External Integrated Summative Assessment, set by the QCTO. Everyone sits it, whatever they were credited for along the way. Passing it is what awards the qualification.

How long does all that take? The qualification is delivered self-paced, so the honest answer depends entirely on the hours committed each week. The study plan does that arithmetic for the eleven knowledge modules and gives you calendar invites for the sessions.

Straight talk

What this pathway is not

Everything above is worth doing. None of it is worth misunderstanding, so here are the limits in plain terms.

Not a 50% discount

Half the content overlapping is not half the qualification signed off, half the time, or half the fee. Overlap is what makes a case for exemption; the provider still has to assess it, and the practical and workplace credits are untouched by it.

Not our decision to make

What gets exempted is decided by Centenary Networks as the accredited provider, and ultimately by the QCTO. The mapping on this page is a starting position for that conversation. It is not an assessment, and no exemption follows from it automatically.

Not a way past the final exam

The EISA cannot be exempted by anything — not by Google, not by RPL, not by years of experience. That is precisely what makes the certificate worth holding.

Where these numbers come from. The module list, codes and credit values are the registered curriculum, taken from the provider's QCTO documents — they are not marketing copy and they are not ours to adjust. The 50% overlap figure is Centenary's own, given to M&T by the provider. Which of the eleven modules sits in each of the two lists above is our reading of the published Google syllabus against the curriculum — useful for planning and for starting the conversation, but it is not an assessment and no exemption follows from it. One further thing worth knowing: Recognition of Prior Learning is not free either — assessment carries its own fee. This route is materially cheaper than studying all 240 credits. It is not a route to no cost.

Thinking about this route?

Talk to HR before you spend anything. Starting the Google certificate on audit costs nothing and commits nothing, and it is the sensible first move either way.
